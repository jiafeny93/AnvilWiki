Every Aniimo you bond with carries a quality badge — and no question dominated closed-beta discussion more than the one this page answers: you just caught an **Elite**… is it worth raising, or do you hold out for **Perfect**? Here is everything beta testing established about Aniimo's four quality tiers, the catch rates behind them, and the one item that turns the right Elite into a Perfect.

Because Aniimo has not launched yet (global release is confirmed for September 16, 2026), the numbers below reflect beta and community data and are subject to change. This guide pairs naturally with our [Inherit system](/guides/aniimo-inherit-system/) and [Prismana variants](/guides/aniimo-prismana/) explainers — quality is the third leg of Aniimo's individuality systems.

## The Four Quality Tiers: Common, Good, Elite, Perfect

Aniimo grades every creature on a four-tier scale, each with its own in-game badge:

| Tier | Badge position | Who it is for |
| :--- | :--- | :--- |
| **Common** | Tier 1 | Filler catches; dex completion |
| **Good** | Tier 2 | Solid early-to-mid roster members |
| **Elite** | Tier 3 | Long-term keeper standard |
| **Perfect** | Tier 4 | The ceiling — endgame and min-max |

Two mechanics wrap around the tiers. First, wild catches are **Unappraised** until you appraise them — you do not know what you have until it is checked. Second, tier is a property of the individual creature, not the species: an Elite [Nimbi](/creatures/aniimo-nimbi/) and a Perfect Nimbi are the same dex entry with different stat ceilings.

## Perfect vs Elite: The Actual Difference

The head-to-head comes down to three things:

1. **Base stats.** Beta footage shows higher quality rolling higher base attributes at the same level. The exact per-tier multipliers are unpublished — but Perfect sits on top of whatever the scaling is.
2. **Rarity.** Elite is roughly a 1-in-14 catch; Perfect is roughly 1-in-125. An Elite is a great catch; a Perfect is an event.
3. **Upgrade ceiling.** Some Elites can *become* Perfect (next section). No tier can skip upward past that — you cannot fruit your way from Good to Elite.

The community consensus from beta is blunt: **only Elite and Perfect are worth serious investment**. Commons and Goods fill the dex and hold gear while you hunt their better-rolled siblings. If you enjoy the loop regardless of optimization, nothing stops you raising a favorite Common — the systems do not gate content by quality.

## Catch Rates by Quality Tier

Community beta data puts the wild-catch distribution at:

| Tier | Approximate rate | Roughly |
| :--- | :---: | :--- |
| Common | ~66% | 2 in 3 |
| Good | ~26% | 1 in 4 |
| Elite | ~7.2% | 1 in 14 |
| Perfect | ~0.8% | 1 in 125 |

The distribution is not fixed. Some capture methods and areas boost high-tier odds — the game tells you when an area is "more likely to yield high-potential Aniimo," which is your cue to farm that zone for keepers. Exact boost values go here once launch data confirms them.

## How to Get a Perfect Aniimo

Three routes are known:

1. **Catch one wild** — the ~0.8% direct roll. High-potential areas tilt the odds.
2. **Upgrade a glistening Elite.** Some Elites carry a glisten/aura effect. That aura is the game flagging the creature as upgradeable — feed it one **Compatibility Fruit** and it becomes Perfect. Each Aniimo can consume only one fruit, and the fruit also grants +1 to each of the creature's two recommended innate potentials. Note the aura is *not* Sparkling — beta footage explicitly showed shiny Elites without the upgrade aura.
3. **Event redemptions.** Limited events can hand out guaranteed Perfects — the Vain Crevice event in the last beta redeemed 99 Pearl Cascades for a Perfect [Irisalis](/creatures/aniimo-irisalis/).

## Quality and the Potential System

Quality is the starting point, not the whole stat story. Each attribute has two potential tracks:

- **Innate Potential** — rolled at capture, fixed… except raisable by items (the Compatibility Fruit's +1s land here).
- **Acquired Potential** — grown after capture with awakening materials, with a per-stat cap.

Innate + Acquired combine up to a cap of **20** per attribute (24 on favored attributes), and a maxed attribute displays a crown. The practical read: quality decides your floor, potential decides your ceiling, and a Perfect you never train loses to a trained Elite. The community-corrected model is *per-attribute and raisable* — older fan wikis describing quality as five fixed, capture-locked grades are out of date.

Quality also moves through the roster via [Inheritance](/guides/aniimo-inherit-system/), which transfers level, quality and experience within a creature family — beta pricing ran about 20,000 gold for an example transfer. Caught a Perfect early-line form? Its quality can follow the family upward as you evolve.

## Do Not Mix These Up With Quality

Three neighboring systems use similar-sounding language:

- **Sparkling** — the shiny axis. Cosmetic rarity at ~1/20,000, independent of quality; 80% Anilog completion unlocks a Sparkling radar. See the [dex guide](/creatures/aniimo-dex/).
- **Rune/gear quality** — equipment grades run Common / Rare / Epic / Legendary. Different ladder, different screens, do not cross-shop the terms.
- **Personality** — the MBTI-style letter traits that add stat percentages and re-roll via feeding. A personality roll is not a quality roll.

Forms and variants ([Nimbi's Cloudmist Form](/guides/aniimo-forms/), [Prismana](/guides/aniimo-prismana/) rainbows) change appearance and sometimes element — they are also orthogonal to quality. A Cloudmist Nimbi still rolls Common-through-Perfect like any other catch.
